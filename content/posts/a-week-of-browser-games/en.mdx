This week I made three little games and went back to Jokarikara, a timing game I [wrote about in October](https://thenkei.github.io/blog/en/posts/jokarikara-first-ai-project). All four run in the browser.

## Ned for Speed

Ned starts out in the Mégamarché car park with his gran’s car, the Biquette, and a reputation to earn. At the lights you can hear its four-cylinder engine rev. Give it too much throttle and the wheels spin while everyone else pulls away. Later, to challenge the local boss, you have to put a piece of jewellery on the bonnet. Lose and the boss keeps it. [Play Ned for Speed](https://thenkei.github.io/ned-for-speed/)

## Parahydox

The same level can be a line to walk along, a cross-section, or a space you can explore in three dimensions. My accomplice is the character I was playing a few seconds ago. I walk onto a pressure plate, wait, then rewind. My clone repeats those keystrokes, holding the plate down while I walk through the door. In some levels I can even climb onto its head. [Play Parahydox](https://thenkei.github.io/parahydox/)

## Putrid Swarm

By day I take the old yellow estate car into the village to scavenge. At night I watch the house on four security cameras. There’s a log rigged above the front step, but I still have to be watching the right camera to drop it in time. Some of the neighbours come back with their old habits intact. The mayor draws reinforcements every time he gives a speech. [Play Putrid Swarm](https://thenkei.github.io/putrid-swarm/)

## Back to Jokarikara

In Jokarikara, a shape grows and spins. I wait until its edge almost touches the outline of the last shape, then tap.

<MotionScene id="jokarikara-containment" />

This week I reworked what happens around that tap. When I miss by a fraction, the game tells me how far I overshot. If a world keeps tripping me up, I can go straight to it in the atlas and practise. And when I get a good run, I can finally share a picture of the shapes I stacked. [Play Jokarikara](https://thenkei.github.io/jokarikara/)
