I just lost a precious hour on an "impossible" case. My `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE` was blithely ignoring obvious duplicates, even though I had protected my table with a uniqueness constraint.

## The Reveal

<ArticleDiagram visualId="postgresql-unique-nulls" />

Here's the trap: by default, PostgreSQL considers that `NULL ≠ NULL`. In other words, show it two rows `(value, NULL)` and it will swear up and down that they are not identical. So much for duplicates!

## The Magic Spell

To avoid tearing your hair out, just add the magic incantation `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` to the unique index:

```sql
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX idx_product_unique
ON products (isin, provider_product_reference)
NULLS NOT DISTINCT;
```

And just like that, PostgreSQL finally accepts that one NULL can be treated as identical to another when checking uniqueness. Result: your upserts work as expected again.

_Worth remembering: this feature has been available since PostgreSQL 15. Take a look at the docs: [Unique Nulls Not Distinct](https://www.postgresql.org/about/featurematrix/detail/unique-nulls-not-distinct/)._
