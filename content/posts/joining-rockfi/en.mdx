Quick announcement: I am joining **RockFi** as a Staff Back-end Engineer.

## How I Got Here

<ArticleDiagram visualId="joining-rockfi" />

It all started at **Polymagine**, a startup where I did R&D on 3D printing and augmented reality. Roughly: making glasses appear on people's faces before that was cool. I then took a detour through **Michelin**, where I learned that the words "industrial production" and "scale" are not to be used lightly.

In 2021, I joined **Forest Admin**. Four years of hypergrowth, during which I went from Full Stack Engineer to Staff Engineer, then Engineering Manager. The tech team doubled to more than 20 engineers. My coffee intake followed a similar curve.

## Why RockFi

First, there is Adrien, a former colleague from Forest Admin. When someone you have already survived a few eventful production releases with is part of the story, you listen a bit more carefully.

Then there are all the people I met during the interviews. Every one of them gave me one more reason to say yes, which is rare enough to be worth mentioning.

And then there is the subject itself: shaking up a field as settled as private wealth management, with some genuinely fresh air. I had that feeling, not so common in a career, of being in the right place at the right time.

## Day One: Data. Well, the Absence of Data

I was put on the data topic straight away. Current state: nothing. No pipeline, no warehouse, not even an old cron script to blame. On the bright side, nobody can hold the existing technical debt against me.

The job is to collect data from our partners (insurers, asset managers…) and consolidate it into a single, centralized view of portfolios.

The schedule does not leave much room for contemplation. The client app is due in a few months, and before then we need to prove part of the promise: enough to grow the network and win new clients.

There are five of us on the tech team. Five people, one app to ship, zero pipelines: an excellent ratio for learning fast.

See you in a few months to find out whether the deadlines survived. Or I did.
