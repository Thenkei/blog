I built Nexus in two days. The v0, anyway. But an already fully featured v0.

To tell that story, though, I need to go back a little further than that weekend.

I spent four and a half years at Forest Admin building internal tools. I learned a lot there. I also developed my own convictions about how those tools should work.

And I have to say it: I didn’t agree with every decision. As a staff engineer and engineering manager, I had been able to argue for my vision, without always feeling heard. That feeling was part of why I left.

Since then, I’d kept my own version of what an internal tool should be in mind. What would eventually become Nexus.

I wasn’t thinking about it every day. But whenever I ran into an internal tools problem, I came back to that idea. I tested my vision against the problem, refined some choices, the abstractions I wanted and where they should live.

Then one weekend, while experimenting with AI, I thought: “Go on, then. Build Nexus for real.”

I started by talking to an AI for an hour. I laid out the main guidelines, explained how I saw the system, its architecture and the decisions I cared about. That gave me a first document, which I read through.

Then I did it again, at a finer level of detail.

For each small component, I described the expected behaviour, the patterns to use, the abstractions I wanted and where I wanted them. It wasn’t code yet. It was just above that level, with choices concrete enough to move into implementation.

About four hours in, I had a first POC that was really convincing.

And I wasn’t spending all my time on it. Mostly, I sent prompts and went off to do something else. Then I came back to see where things stood and started another iteration.

Very quickly, I was able to run several workstreams in parallel. The broad outline was there, followed by the details of each component. I had enough to move forward on several parts of the system.

By the end of that weekend, I had my v0 of Nexus. I then presented my progress to all the teams at RockFi.

In about three weeks, I had recreated 100% of what we had built over two years in Retool, our internal tool. That’s the version I describe in [Built With Agents, Owned by Us](../posts/internal-tools-built-with-agents).

I even added extra screens and use cases. I wanted to show what I could do with the UX, including reusing assets already present on the sites we build and offer. I wanted to show concretely what that experience could look like in our internal tools.

That’s how I built Nexus in two days. With four and a half years of experience at Forest Admin and an idea I’d kept working on since, whenever another internal tools problem came up.

By the time I finally started, I already had a lot to tell the AI.
