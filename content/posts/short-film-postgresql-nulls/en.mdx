In January, I wrote [a quick note about an `ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`](../posts/postgresql-unique-nulls) that ignored obvious duplicates even though the table had a uniqueness constraint. One of the index's columns could be NULL, and by default, PostgreSQL considers that `NULL ≠ NULL`.

Here is the same story in 41 seconds.

<ArticleVideo id="short-film-nulls">
  The NULL trap, second episode of the short films.
</ArticleVideo>

In the film, the unique index is a doorman. It recognises a duplicate when every value is there. When the reference is NULL, it lets in two `(value, NULL)` rows that it does not find identical, and the duplicates pile up.

The fix is one line: add `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` to the unique index. The doorman then recognises the duplicate, and the upsert updates the row instead of adding one. It is available since PostgreSQL 15.

The original article gives the complete index: [ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE with nullable columns](../posts/postgresql-unique-nulls).
