In 2021, I described [how we scaled our CI at Forest Admin](../posts/scaling-ci-github-actions-forest-admin). With more than 95% test coverage, a single runner took 45 minutes to run the tests: longer than a lunch break.

Here is the same story in 45 seconds.

<ArticleVideo id="short-film-ci">
  The CI that outlasted lunch, third episode of the short films.
</ArticleVideo>

The film keeps the three problems the article describes once the tests are split across ten jobs: a network blip on one worker fails the whole pipeline, a teardown that leaves a record behind makes one chunk flaky, and a reconciliation job has to wait for every chunk to assemble the coverage.

It also keeps the two rules that made the split hold: leave no trace between tests, and spread the tests by execution time rather than by number of files.

The original article gives the details of the GitHub Actions setup and of merging the reports: [Scaling CI at Forest Admin](../posts/scaling-ci-github-actions-forest-admin).
