For as long as I can remember, two directions have drawn me in: far above us and deep beneath our feet.

On one side, the stars, the constellations, and everything else in the sky. On the other, volcanoes. The two may seem unrelated, but I think it is the same curiosity.

## Looking up

I have watched the stars since I was a child, at home, in the countryside. I still do, regularly. I look for constellations, try to recognise their shapes, and easily get absorbed by everything in the night sky.

I never needed this curiosity to be useful. Looking was enough. The distances fascinate me, and so does the time the light from those stars took to reach us. The more I learn, the more questions I have.

That fascination never really left me. It is probably why the Rocket theme of this blog feels so much like me.

## Finding constellations

What I like about constellations is that at first you only see points of light. Then you learn to tell them apart, name them, and find your bearings.

I think part of my taste for engineering comes from there too: look at something complex, find a few stable points, then try to understand how they relate to each other.

Of course, I did not look at the sky in those terms as a child. I looked because it drew me in. The rest came later.

## Looking beneath our feet

On Earth, the phenomenon that has always fascinated me most lies almost in the opposite direction: volcanoes.

They make visible forces that usually stay out of our sight. They reshape landscapes, and I find them both beautiful and worrying. The ones I have seen in person are extinct: the Chaîne des Puys and the Cantal.

Perhaps that is why I was good at biology and Earth science at school. Or perhaps I was good at those subjects because they already fascinated me. I no longer know which came first.

## The same curiosity

In both cases, I like trying to understand what I cannot see directly, from what we manage to observe.

Astronomy makes me look beyond Earth. Volcanoes remind me how active and precious this Earth is.

I have never seen those two directions as contradictory. Looking at the sky does not make me want to leave our planet.

## Why this logbook

That is what I like about this logbook: the Rocket theme becomes more than an appearance. It gives access to more personal texts, about subjects I already loved as a child.

Stars and volcanoes were there long before my first program, and they interest me just as much today.
