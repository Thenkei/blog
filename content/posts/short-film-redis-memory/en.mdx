In 2021, I wrote the [post-mortem of an outage at Forest Admin](../posts/redis-memory-exhaustion-post-mortem). A single Redis instance held the cache, the job queues, the sessions and the anti brute-force guard. One weekend the cache filled the memory, and everything else went down with it.

Here is the same story in 44 seconds.

<ArticleVideo id="short-film-redis">
  The Redis outage, first episode of the short films.
</ArticleVideo>

The film keeps the article's three causes: no alert on Redis memory, one instance for uses that had nothing to do with each other, and data that stayed too long. It also keeps the fixes: a separate instance for the cache, the queues and the sessions, shorter TTLs, stale data cleaned up and compressed payloads.

The engineer's four lines come from the article: Redis was fast and reliable, no day looked abnormal, the cache was never meant to be critical, and data from deleted projects was not purged.

The original article gives the context and the details of the fixes: [Post-Mortem: The Day Redis Reached its Limits](../posts/redis-memory-exhaustion-post-mortem).
