At [RockFi](https://www.rockfi.fr/), I have found myself changing hats ("changer de casquette", as we say in French). My roots are in backend engineering, but since I joined, most of my time goes into data.

It was not a career plan. In my previous jobs, we produced our own data, so we controlled it end to end. At RockFi, a large part of what matters comes from outside: insurers, asset managers, price providers… Consolidating all of it properly is, quite literally, the product.

## Ten Sources, Ten Formats (and That Is Not the Worst Part)

At the start, we had around ten sources, and roughly as many formats. There are more today. 😅

Against all expectations, formats are not the real problem. An exotic CSV can be parsed. What hurts is the **transmission modes**, and above all the way each partner fixes its own mistakes.

Some use reversals: they send a row that cancels the old one, then a new, correct row. Others rewrite history: yesterday's row simply changed this morning, without warning. Both approaches are defensible; the real sport is making them coexist in the same table.

So the job is less about reading files than understanding intentions, which are not always documented. A good share of the work looks like archaeology: dig up a feed, date it, and try to guess what its author had in mind.

## Orchestrating It All with Dagster

My days now revolve around [Dagster](https://dagster.io/). It was our CTO's choice: he wanted a newer tool, betting that it would evolve faster than the established alternatives.

Our pipelines follow the **medallion architecture**:

- **Bronze**: the data exactly as the partner sent it, reversals and rewrites included. No judgment, just archiving.
- **Silver**: cleaned, deduplicated, and reconciled data.
- **Gold**: what the teams and the product actually consume.

So that everyone talks about the same numbers, we are building a semantic layer with **dbt**. We also looked at **Cube**, which did not meet our needs, so we set it aside.

## Next: A Proper Data Warehouse

The pipelines are running and the first governance models are in place. The next step is a proper data warehouse. **Snowflake** and **ClickHouse** are both on the table.

_Update (2026): it will be Snowflake. ClickHouse might join the stack one day, for cases where latency really matters (real-time analytics). To be continued._

As for the hat, the takeaway is simple. Data calls for a different reflex than backend work: you no longer control the source, only what you do with it. And when ten partners each have their own idea of what "fixing a row" means, you pick up that reflex fairly quickly.
